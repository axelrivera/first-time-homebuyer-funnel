# Team and Funnel Scaling — Revenue Models and Growth Decisions

This doc answers three operational questions that aren't covered by the funnel build specs:

1. How do you actually operate the LM1/LM2 FTHB funnel as a solo agent?
2. When do you grow the team around the existing funnel (TC, 2nd agent, 3rd agent)?
3. When do you expand to additional funnels (Sellers, Relocation, Move-Up), and in what order?

Everything else in [docs/](.) describes *what the funnel does*. This doc describes *how to run the business around it* and the revenue you should expect at each stage. The numbers are planning estimates calibrated to industry benchmarks and the funnel's own probability table from [09-deal-pipeline-stages.md](09-deal-pipeline-stages.md); they are not promises. They get tightened against real submission and closing data once ~50 Deals have moved through the pipeline.

---

## Recommended path at a glance

A 30-month build sequence from solo to the efficient endpoint. Each row is a stable stage you can hold for months before triggering the next move.

| Stage | Calendar | Selling agents | Support | Funnels live | Annual transactions | Annual gross GCI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Year 1 H1 (months 1–6) | 1 (solo) | None | FTHB | 4–10 | $45K–$113K |
| 2 | Year 1 H2 (months 7–12) | 1 | TC | FTHB | 15–25 | $169K–$281K |
| 3 | Year 2 H1 | 2 | TC | FTHB | 30–50 | $338K–$563K |
| 4 | Year 2 H2 | 3 | TC | FTHB + Sellers | 55–85 | $646K–$1,019K |
| 5 | Year 3 H1 | 4 | TC + content lead | FTHB + Sellers + Reloc | 95–135 | $1,163K–$1,675K |
| 6 | Year 3 H2+ | 5 | TC + content lead + showing assistant | All four | 175–225 | $2,188K–$2,813K |

Going faster than this means burning capital on infrastructure ahead of demand. Going slower means leaving compounding funnel ROI on the table once content matures.

## Core assumptions

Every number in this doc is derived from this list. Change one and the rest move proportionally.

### Funnel conversion (from [09-deal-pipeline-stages.md](09-deal-pipeline-stages.md))

- **Quiz Taken → Closed Won**: 10% over an 18-month window. This is the headline conversion rate.
- **Tier A + Tier B share of LM1 submissions**: 60% (the rest are Tier C Leads that contribute marginally in iteration 1).
- **Quiz Taken → Received Roadmap**: ~50% (derived from the stage probabilities).
- **Received Roadmap → BSS Booked**: ~50%.
- **BSS Booked → Closed Won**: ~40%.

### Per-agent transaction capacity

| Configuration | Annual transactions per agent | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Solo agent, no support | 15–20 | Admin and coordination eat ~40% of working hours |
| Agent + shared TC | 25–30 | TC absorbs Under Contract → Closing admin and BBA/MLS paperwork |
| Agent + TC + showing assistant | 35–45 | Showing assistant absorbs 8–15 hrs/week of property tours and logistics |
| Agent + TC + showing assistant + ISA | 50–70 | Mega-team territory; out of scope for this doc |

### Ticket size and commission

- **FTHB average sale**: $450K. Source: project memory ([feedback_anchor_price_450k](../CLAUDE.md)).
- **Buyer-side commission**: 2.5% = **$11,250 gross GCI per FTHB transaction**.
- **Diversified mix average GCI** (when all four funnels are live): ~$12,500 per transaction. Move-Up buy and Relocation push the average up.
- **Brokerage split**: assume 20% to brokerage. Adjust for your actual split.

### Top-of-funnel scaling

LM1 submissions per month, organic content only (no paid ads, per [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md) "do not build / do not recommend" rules), assuming consistent content output of 25–30 pieces/week across IG / FB / YouTube / TikTok plus 1 blog post/week.

| Team size | Slow start (mo. 1–3) | Steady state (mo. 4–12) | Momentum (year 2) | Compounding (year 3+) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 agent | 5–15 | 20–40 | 40–80 | 70–130 |
| 2 agents | 12–28 | 35–65 | 70–130 | 110–200 |
| 3 agents | 18–40 | 50–90 | 100–180 | 160–290 |
| 4 agents | 25–55 | 65–115 | 130–240 | 220–400 |
| 5 agents | 30–65 | 80–140 | 160–290 | 270–490 |

Sub-linear scaling because additional agents share content production and have overlapping social spheres. Content compounds (blog SEO, YouTube library), so year 2 and year 3 LM1 volumes pull dramatically ahead of year 1 with no additional content effort.

### Support staff cost (annual, fully loaded)

| Role | Cost | Capacity served |
|---|---|---|
| Transaction Coordinator (TC) | $55K–$65K | ~150 transactions/year before quality erodes |
| Showing assistant (junior licensed agent) | $40K–$55K base + small commission split | 2–3 senior buyer's agents |
| Content lead | $55K–$75K | All four funnels' content production |

## Part 1: Operating the FTHB funnel solo

This is months 1–12 (roughly). One person, no TC, no second agent. The funnel is designed for this configuration: a single Pipedrive owner, a single Google Calendar for BSS bookings, no lead routing logic.

### Realistic monthly cadence

- **5–10 warm DMs per day** to your sphere and recent acquaintances (per [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md) "Working voice and the Grand Slam Offer test"). 25–50 DMs/week.
- **3 social posts per week minimum** across the platforms you already use (FB / IG / YouTube). Don't start TikTok solo unless you genuinely enjoy it; the format is demanding.
- **1 blog post every 2 weeks** is realistic solo. 1/week is achievable but at the cost of other work.
- **BSS calendar**: hold 2 half-days per week (8–10 slots). You won't fill them for months; protect the time anyway so when bookings come you don't scramble.
- **Pipedrive hygiene**: 30 minutes daily reviewing new Deals, replying to inbound, advancing manual stages. The funnel does most routing; you do the human follow-up.

### What you do *not* do as a solo agent

- No paid ads. Not just because of [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md) rules. At <30 LM1/mo organic, you can't tell what's working and ads will hide signal.
- No Tier C live outreach. The 9-email Tier C automation does the work. Manual review only after the automation ends.
- No third lead magnet. Strictly LM1 + LM2.
- No second funnel build. You do not have the bandwidth.

### Solo revenue model (FTHB only, no TC)

| Phase | Months | LM1/mo | Closings/year (uncapped) | Capacity | Actual closings | Gross GCI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Slow start | 1–3 | 5–15 | 4–11 | 15–20 | 4–10 | $45K–$113K |
| Steady state | 4–12 | 20–40 | 14–29 | 15–20 | 14–20 | **$158K–$225K** |

Year 1 annualized: roughly **15–25 closings, $169K–$281K gross GCI**. Net of brokerage split (~20%) and operating costs: ~$125K–$215K to you.

### Trigger to move to Stage 2 (add a TC)

Move when **all three** are true:

1. **2+ closings/month sustained for 3+ months**. Not aspirational. Already happening.
2. **Admin debt is slipping**. Examples: lender follow-up emails sitting 2+ days, inspection scheduling reactive instead of proactive, post-close gift / referral asks falling off because there's no time.
3. **You have ~$60K of forecastable annual GCI to commit to a TC salary**. At ~2.5 closings/month × 12 = 30 closings × $11,250 = $338K gross → ~$270K after brokerage → covers a TC plus your income.

Don't add the TC earlier. Below 15 closings/year the TC cost outpaces the admin pain.

## Part 2: Growing the FTHB funnel

Three growth moves on the team side, each gated by specific triggers.

### Stage 2: Solo + TC

**What changes:**

- TC handles Under Contract → Closing admin, BBA management, MLS/portal hygiene, lender follow-up, post-close coordination.
- You gain ~20% of your week back. Spend half on more transactions, half on more content (this is where blog frequency goes to 1/week).
- No funnel modifications. TC operates within your single Pipedrive ownership.

**Revenue model:**

| Phase | LM1/mo | Closings/year | Capacity | Actual | Gross GCI | Net of TC + brokerage |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Steady state | 30–50 | 22–36 | 25–30 | 22–30 | $248K–$338K | $138K–$210K (your take) |
| Year 2 momentum | 50–90 | 36–65 | 25–30 | **25–30** (capped) | $281K–$338K | $165K–$210K |

You hit capacity in year 2 momentum. The funnel is now producing 30–60% more demand than you can serve. Time to decide: 2nd agent (Stage 3) or 2nd funnel.

### Stage 3: 2 agents + TC

**Trigger:**

- Sustained 4+ closings/month combined forecast
- Content engine producing 60+ LM1/month consistently
- BSS calendar at 80%+ utilization for 8+ consecutive weeks
- You have specific funnel modifications ready (see below)

**Funnel modifications required** before the second agent starts:

1. **Lead-owner routing in Make.com.** Add a round-robin rule that sets `owner_id` on the created Deal/Lead. Round-robin is simplest. Sphere-based (route by zip code, language preference, or quiz signal) is sharper but takes longer to spec.
2. **Two BSS Calendar slugs** (or a Calendly team page with round-robin). Result page passes through to whichever agent was assigned.
3. **Owner field on the LM2 webhook** so standalone-roadmap path also distributes new Deals.
4. **Email transactional copy parameterized** with the owning agent's name, signature, reply-to.
5. **Pipedrive automation routing.** All four automations stay shared; enrollment runs in the context of the owning agent so replies route correctly.

Without these, the second agent has nothing to own and you end up doing all the funnel work anyway.

**Revenue model:**

| Phase | LM1/mo | Closings/year | Capacity | Actual | Gross GCI | Per-agent net |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Steady state | 35–65 | 25–47 | 50–60 | 25–47 | $281K–$529K | $112K–$212K each |
| Year 2 momentum | 70–130 | 50–94 | 50–60 | 50–60 (capped) | $563K–$675K | $225K–$270K each |

Per-agent net is best at this stage. Two agents share the TC cost and the funnel's lead volume neatly. Adding the 3rd agent only makes sense once volume saturates this configuration.

### Stage 3+: 3 agents + TC (still FTHB only)

If you stay on FTHB only and don't add the Sellers funnel, capacity reaches 75–90 closings/year. But by this point the content engine is producing 100–180 LM1/month, which means the third agent runs out of marginal demand fast. **The better move at this scale is to add the Sellers funnel rather than the third agent.** See Part 3.

## Part 3: Expanding to multiple funnels

Three additional funnels are in scope: **Sellers**, **Relocation**, **Move-Up**. Each gets its own namespace per [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md) "Funnel namespace convention": `seller`, `reloc`, `moveup`. Sequence matters because each funnel has different build cost, demand profile, and cross-funnel leverage.

### Why sequence in this order

1. **Sellers second** — every FTHB client becomes a seller eventually. You need this for client lifecycle even if your top-of-funnel was free. Listings also produce buyer leads (sign calls, open houses, portal inquiries) that backfill the FTHB funnel at zero acquisition cost.
2. **Relocation third** — heaviest content overlap with FTHB so build cost is lowest. The LM1 readiness quiz adapts with 2–3 added questions about timing-to-move and home equity in current state. The agent's PR → Orlando story is a credibility anchor that's wasted without a funnel to channel it.
3. **Move-Up fourth** — highest GCI per family (dual-sided commission) but highest client-service complexity. Coupled sell-and-buy transactions can implode each other. You want established systems before you take this on.

### Per-funnel build cost

After the FTHB infrastructure exists (Make.com routing, Pipedrive custom fields, namespace conventions, email templates), each new funnel reuses most of it.

| Funnel | Build hours | Calendar time | What's distinctive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sellers | 150–250 | 8–12 weeks | New audience, new lead magnet (home valuation tool), seller-side custom fields |
| Relocation | 100–180 | 6–8 weeks | Adapted LM1 quiz, virtual tour workflow, out-of-state lender intro process |
| Move-Up | 250–400 | 10–16 weeks | Coupled-transaction tracker, sell-first vs buy-first BSS variants, bridge loan intros |

Build hours are agent-or-contractor time. At an internal time value of $75–$150/hour, total: **$40K–$125K** of investment across all three funnels.

### When to add Sellers (Stage 4)

**Trigger:** at least one of:

- You have 20+ FTHB clients who closed 18+ months ago and are starting to ask "what's my home worth now?"
- Listing-side referrals leaking to other agents because you have nothing to send them
- Stage 3 hit (2 agents + TC at sustained capacity)

**Team configuration:** 3 agents + TC, one of them gravitating to listings as a specialty.

**Per-funnel demand at this stage:**

| Funnel | LM1/month | Tier A+B | Close rate | Avg GCI | Annual closings | Annual GCI |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FTHB | 50–90 | 60% | 10% | $11,250 | 36–65 | $405K–$731K |
| Sellers | 40–80 | 50% | 4% | $11,875 | 10–19 | $119K–$226K |
| **Total** | **90–170** | | | | **46–84** | **$524K–$957K** |

Sellers close rate is much lower than FTHB (4% vs 10%) because "What's my home worth?" attracts a much wider audience including many tire-kickers. Volume is high; conversion is selective.

**Revenue model, Stage 4:**

| Phase | Combined closings/year | Capacity (3 agents) | Actual | Gross GCI | Total team net |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Steady state | 46–84 | 75–90 | 46–84 | $524K–$957K | $379K–$706K |
| Year 2 momentum | 90–150 | 75–90 | **75–90** (capped) | $880K–$1,055K | $644K–$784K |

Per-agent net: ~$215K–$261K at steady, ~$215K–$261K at momentum. The team is leaving demand on the table once capped, but per-agent earnings hold.

### When to add Relocation (Stage 5)

**Trigger:** at least one of:

- 5+ inbound out-of-state inquiries per month from blog or YouTube content
- The PR migration angle is producing Spanish-language inquiries you can't channel into the FTHB funnel cleanly
- Stage 4 sustained for 6+ months

**Team configuration:** 4 agents + TC + content lead. The content lead role becomes critical here because three funnels produce 25–40 hours/week of content work; agents contributing 6–8 hours each starts to hurt quality and consistency.

**Revenue model, Stage 5:**

| Funnel | LM1/month | Annual closings | Annual GCI |
|---|---|---|---|
| FTHB | 65–115 | 47–83 | $529K–$934K |
| Sellers | 50–100 | 12–24 | $143K–$285K |
| Relocation | 30–60 | 28–56 | $350K–$700K |
| **Total** | **145–275** | **87–163** | **$1,022K–$1,919K** |

Capacity at 4 agents + TC + content lead: ~100–120 transactions. Hits cap in year 2.

| Phase | Combined closings/year | Capacity | Actual | Gross GCI | Per-agent net |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Steady state | 87–163 | 100–120 | 87–120 | $1,022K–$1,425K | $204K–$285K |
| Year 2 momentum | 160–280 | 100–120 | **100–120** (capped) | $1,250K–$1,500K | $250K–$300K |

### When to add Move-Up (Stage 6)

**Trigger:** all of:

- Team has handled 50+ FTHB closings and 20+ listings
- TC has experience coordinating timeline-coupled transactions (a few move-up families served via the existing funnels by referral)
- Stage 5 sustained for 6+ months with capacity routinely at 90%+

**Team configuration:** 5 agents + TC + showing assistant + content lead. The 5th agent specializes in Move-Up buy side (the most complex client work); the showing assistant absorbs FTHB volume work to free senior agents for higher-ticket transactions.

**Revenue model, Stage 6 — the efficient endpoint:**

| Funnel | LM1/month | Annual transactions (uncapped) | Annual GCI |
|---|---|---|---|
| FTHB | 80–150 | 60–108 | $675K–$1,215K |
| Sellers | 100–200 | 60–120 listings | $713K–$1,425K |
| Relocation | 40–80 | 37–75 | $463K–$938K |
| Move-Up | 30–60 | 57–114 (38–76 families × 1.5 sides avg) | $713K–$1,425K |
| **Total** | **250–490** | **214–417** | **$2.6M–$5.0M** |

Capacity at 5 agents + TC + showing assistant: 175–225 transactions. So actual capture is roughly 50–80% of uncapped demand.

| Phase | Uncapped transactions | Capacity | Actual | Gross GCI (mix-optimized) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Steady state | 130–250 | 175–225 | 130–225 | $1,625K–$2,813K |
| Compounding year 3+ | 300–500 | 175–225 | **175–225** (capped) | $2,188K–$2,813K |

### What the comparison reveals

**Per-agent earnings stay roughly flat from Stage 3 through Stage 5** (around $215K–$270K) even as total revenue triples. Each new selling agent captures roughly their own keep, plus marginal gains from funnel diversification.

**Stage 6 jumps per-agent earnings 40%+** because the showing assistant unlocks the high-volume FTHB seats and Move-Up dual-sided commission lifts the per-transaction average.

**Stages 4 and 5 are transition stages, not destinations.** They're worth running through deliberately because the operational learning matters (lead routing, multi-funnel content, listing-side workflow), but the economic argument for living in them long-term is weak. Either commit to Stage 6 or retreat to Stage 3 with a tighter FTHB focus.

## Part 5: Per-agent earnings and team satisfaction

A team where agents are unhappy is a team that loses agents. Real estate has 100% commission portability; nothing stops an agent from leaving for another brokerage if the seat under-delivers. Each stage needs to make the math work for the agents in it.

### Per-agent net GCI by stage

| Stage | Configuration | Per-agent net | What makes it work |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Solo | $135K–$225K | You set your own hours; no team overhead |
| 2 | Solo + TC | $138K–$210K | TC absorbs admin; you take all transactions |
| 3 | 2 agents + TC | $195K–$240K | Capacity used well; clear sphere ownership |
| 4 | 3 agents + TC | $215K–$261K | One agent gravitates to listings; clear specialization |
| 5 | 4 agents + TC + CL | $219K–$269K | Content lead removes the dreaded content load |
| 6 | 5 agents + TC + SA + CL | $314K–$414K | Specialization + showing assistant + mix uplift |

### Agent-satisfaction factors at each stage

**Stage 3 (2 agents + TC).** Each agent owns half the lead routing and handles all stages of their own deals. Equal partnership feel. Risk: if one agent has a slow month, the other resents the shared TC cost. Mitigation: TC cost shared 50/50 regardless of transaction split.

**Stage 4 (3 agents + TC).** One agent (often the most senior or the one with marketing instinct) becomes the listing specialist. Listings have a longer cycle and bigger lump-sum payouts; buyer-side has steadier volume and lower per-transaction GCI. Pay structure needs to acknowledge this so the listing agent doesn't feel under-paid during long marketing periods.

**Stage 5 (4 agents + TC + content lead).** The content lead is the most important hire here. Without it, four agents are each spending 8 hours/week on content and resenting it. With it, agents do 1 hour/week of on-camera or quote contribution and the content lead does the production. Quality goes up, agent satisfaction goes up.

**Stage 6 (5 agents + TC + SA + CL).** Specialization peaks:

- Listing specialist (Sellers + Move-Up sell side)
- Move-Up buy specialist (dual-side coordination)
- Relocation specialist (virtual workflow, often Spanish-fluent)
- 2 FTHB / general buyer agents (highest volume, the showing assistant supports them most)

Each agent owns a domain. Earnings differentiate naturally by ticket size, but volume balances against ticket size, so total earnings end up roughly comparable across the team.

### What makes agents unhappy on this team

Watch for these. Each is solvable but only if caught early.

| Failure mode | What it looks like | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Lead-routing favoritism | One agent feels routed all the Tier C / dead leads | Document the round-robin rule in writing; review monthly |
| Listing-buy imbalance | Listing agent goes 6 weeks without a close because all listings are mid-marketing | Smooth pay via team draw against trailing 6-month earnings |
| Content load resentment | Agents asked to do content but not given prep time | Block calendar time for content explicitly; respect it |
| Showing assistant overload | One showing assistant supports too many agents, becomes a bottleneck | Cap at 2 senior agents per SA; add a second SA when needed |
| BSS calendar burnout | One agent always volunteered for weekend BSS slots | Rotate weekend duty; track who's holding what |
| Promotion ladder absent | Junior agents stay junior; nobody knows what "next" looks like | Document the ladder: SA → buyer's agent → specialist → listing agent |

### Pay structure considerations

Out of scope for this doc to prescribe a specific split structure, but the model assumes:

- Agent commission split with brokerage: 80% to agent (assumed in this doc; varies by brokerage)
- Within team: team lead takes 20–40% of agent's portion in exchange for funnel-generated leads and admin support
- Net to agent: roughly 48–64% of gross commission

For an agent doing 25 FTHB closings/year at $11,250 GCI each = $281K gross → roughly $135K–$180K to the agent. Per-agent net earnings in the tables above are approximations of this number.

## Decision framework: add agent, add funnel, or add support?

At each scaling decision you have three options. Each wins under different conditions.

### Add an agent when

- Capacity utilization is sustained above 80% for 8+ weeks
- Current funnels are producing more qualified Deals than the team can serve (specifically: Pre-Approved & Searching stage is sitting at >20 active Deals per agent)
- BSS calendar is at >75% utilization
- You have lead routing infrastructure in place (Stage 3+ requirements)

**What it solves:** capacity ceiling. **What it costs:** new agent ramp time (3–6 months to full productivity), team coordination overhead.

### Add a funnel when

- Capacity utilization is below 60% (you have unused agent time)
- An adjacent audience is showing up unsolicited (relocation inquiries, listing referral asks)
- Current funnel's content has matured and is producing diminishing returns on incremental posts
- You have 6+ months of operating budget to fund the build

**What it solves:** demand diversification, average ticket lift, lifetime client capture. **What it costs:** 100–400 build hours per funnel, 6–16 weeks calendar time.

### Add a support role when

- A specific repetitive task is consuming agent time disproportionately to its value
- TC: admin debt accumulating, post-close coordination slipping
- Showing assistant: agents spending >15 hrs/week on showings, not enough on BSS/strategy
- Content lead: content quality dropping or posting frequency slipping

**What it solves:** per-agent productivity. **What it costs:** $45K–$75K/year per role, plus the management overhead of supervising support staff.

### When two or more triggers fire simultaneously

Prioritize in this order:

1. **Support role first** (it multiplies existing capacity)
2. **Funnel next** (it diversifies demand without adding headcount)
3. **Agent last** (the most expensive option and the slowest to ramp)

The exception: if BSS calendar capacity is the binding constraint (every agent's calendar is full), you need an agent, not a support role. Support can't take BSS calls.

## Bottlenecks to watch

Each one becomes binding at a different scale. Knowing which is binding next lets you fix the right thing.

### BSS calendar capacity

Each agent sustainably runs ~10 BSS slots/week (5 hours of calendar + 5 hours of prep, notes, and follow-up). Team capacity scales linearly with selling agent count:

| Agents | BSS slots/week | BSS slots/month | LM1 volume that fills it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 10 | 40 | ~160 LM1/mo (at 25% LM1 → BSS) |
| 2 | 20 | 80 | ~320 LM1/mo |
| 3 | 30 | 120 | ~480 LM1/mo |
| 4 | 40 | 160 | ~640 LM1/mo |
| 5 | 50 | 200 | ~800 LM1/mo |

At the efficient endpoint (5 agents, ~350 LM1/mo midpoint demand), BSS utilization is ~44%. Comfortable headroom.

### Tier C pool growth

The 9-email Tier C automation runs over 8 weeks and then drops contacts to manual review. By year 2 with multi-funnel volume, the Tier C pool is 1,000–3,000 contacts. The current funnel design has no plan for them beyond the initial 8-week drip.

**When this becomes binding:** ~12 months after the second funnel ships. **Fix:** decide whether to extend Tier C into a year-round educational newsletter, and whether Tier C contacts are owned by a specific agent or pooled.

### Content production load

Realistic content output at each stage:

| Stage | Content hours/week (team total) | Per agent |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 12–18 | 12–18 |
| 2 | 14–20 | 14–20 |
| 3 | 18–28 | 9–14 |
| 4 | 22–32 | 7–11 |
| 5 | 25–40 | 6–10 + content lead full-time |
| 6 | 25–40 | 5–8 + content lead full-time |

Past 25 hrs/week of content production, a dedicated content lead pays for itself in retained agent productivity.

### Transaction Coordinator capacity

A single TC supports up to ~150 transactions/year before service quality erodes (lender follow-ups slip, addenda go untracked, post-close coordination becomes reactive).

| Stage | Transactions/yr | TC count |
|---|---|---|
| 1–4 | up to 90 | 1 |
| 5 | 100–120 | 1 (comfortable) |
| 6 | 175–225 | 1 with a part-time second TC, or 2 part-time |

### Listing-side workflow (introduced at Stage 4)

Listing transactions have different operational shape than buyer-side: more upfront marketing time, less mid-stage coordination, lump-sum payout at close. The TC's process needs to fork:

- **Buyer side**: BBA → MLS searches → showings → offer → contingencies → close
- **Listing side**: listing agreement → photos / staging → MLS entry → marketing → offers → contingencies → close

A TC trained only on buyer-side will fumble listings. Budget 2–4 weeks of cross-training time when the Sellers funnel ships.

## Re-calibration cadence

These numbers are starting points. They get tightened against real data.

| When | What to review | Why |
|---|---|---|
| After 25 FTHB closings | Quiz Taken → Won conversion rate | The 10% in [09-deal-pipeline-stages.md](09-deal-pipeline-stages.md) is an industry benchmark; your actual will land within 5–15% |
| After 6 months of consistent content | LM1 submissions/month at your specific content cadence | Top-of-funnel scaling estimates here assume "25–30 pieces/week of educational content"; calibrate against what you actually produce |
| After 12 months | Tier mix (A+B vs C) | The 60% Tier A+B assumption depends on your audience targeting; paid traffic or broad social would push it lower |
| Annually | Per-agent transaction capacity | If your agents sustainably run higher than 30 transactions/year with TC support, the model under-estimates capacity; if lower, the opposite |
| Before each stage transition | All the numbers in Core Assumptions | Re-derive the next stage's revenue model with your actual data, not the planning estimates |

Pipedrive's Funnel report ([09-deal-pipeline-stages.md:319](09-deal-pipeline-stages.md#L319)) is the primary instrument. Run it monthly once you have ≥10 Deals through.

## What this doc doesn't cover

- **Cost of acquisition for paid channels.** Out of scope per [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md) "do not build / do not recommend" rules. If paid ads enter scope later, the LM1 volume numbers in this doc don't apply; paid traffic has different conversion and tier-mix dynamics.
- **Specific commission split negotiations** with brokerage, team agents, or the TC. Assumed 20% to brokerage and 60–80% to agent gross. Your actuals will differ.
- **Tax treatment** of agent earnings (1099 vs W-2, S-corp distributions, etc.). Talk to a CPA.
- **Hiring and management practices.** This doc says "add a 3rd agent at Stage 4"; it doesn't tell you how to find and onboard one.
- **Branding, brokerage selection, or franchise model decisions.** All assumed stable.
- **Geographic expansion.** Numbers are calibrated to Orlando metro per [CLAUDE.md](../CLAUDE.md) market context. Tampa, Jacksonville, or out-of-metro expansion would need re-derivation.
