# FTHB Roadmap - Day 09 - Pre-approval

**Subject:** Pre-approval vs prequalification (most first-time buyers get this wrong)

**Preview:** One is a guess. The other is the letter sellers actually take seriously.

**Send:** Day 9 (7 days after N1)

---

```
Hey *|FIRST_NAME|*,

Most first-time buyers use the words "pre-qualification" and "pre-approval" interchangeably. They aren't the same thing, and sellers know the difference.

Pre-qualification: the lender asks you a few questions, you self-report income and debts, they hand you a ballpark number. No credit pull. No documents verified. Useful for budgeting. Not useful for making offers.

Pre-approval: the lender pulls your credit, verifies your W-2s, paystubs, tax returns, and asset statements, then runs the file through actual underwriting standards. You get a letter with a specific loan amount, a rate range, and conditions. This is the document sellers want attached to your offer.

Listing agents triage offers by who has a real pre-approval and who doesn't. A pre-qual letter signals you're shopping. A pre-approval signals you're ready.

Five questions worth asking before you pick a lender:

1. What's your rate today on a 30-year fixed for a credit profile like mine?
2. What origination, processing, and underwriting fees do you charge?
3. Do you service the loan after closing, or sell it to another servicer?
4. From contract to clear-to-close, how many days does your underwriting actually take?
5. What happens if my credit score dings between pre-approval and closing?

Talk to at least two lenders. As an illustration only: a 0.25% rate difference on a roughly $450K, 30-year fixed loan works out to about $75 a month, or roughly $27,000 over the full term. Your numbers will differ based on actual rate, loan amount, term, and how long you hold the loan; treat this as a sanity-check on why shopping matters, not as a quote.

A note on builder's preferred lenders. Sometimes they're the best deal in the room because the builder is buying down the rate or covering closing costs. Sometimes they're the worst because the markup gets buried in fees. Don't rule them out automatically. Don't take them automatically either. Compare.

If you want a starting point, reply 'lender list' and I'll send a short list of Orlando lenders worth calling. I receive no compensation, gift, or thing of value from any of them. Shop at least three independently.

-- Axel
```
