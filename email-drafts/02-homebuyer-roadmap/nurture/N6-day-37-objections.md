# FTHB Roadmap - Day 37 - Objections

**Subject:** Wait for rates or buy now: the actual math

**Preview:** The two objections that cost first-time buyers more than the rate itself.

**Send:** Day 37 (7 days after N5)

---

```
Hey *|FIRST_NAME|*,

Two objections come up more than any others when a first-time buyer stalls. Both feel cautious. Both are usually wrong.

Objection one: I should wait for rates to drop.

Run the math.

Today at 6.5% on a $450K home, 5% down conventional, your monthly P&I is about $2,700. If rates drop to 5.5% 18 months from now (which is optimistic), your monthly P&I on the same house would be about $2,425. You'd save $275 a month.

Over those 18 months of waiting, here is what you actually spend: 18 months of rent. At $2,000 a month, that's $36,000 paid to a landlord, building zero equity. Even if rates drop exactly when you bet they will, you spent $36K to save $275 a month.

And refinancing exists. If you buy at 6.5% now and rates drop to 5.5% in a year, you refinance. You don't lose the house you wanted to wait for the rate on. The house you wanted is still yours.

The cleaner framing: marry the house, date the rate.

Objection two: I need to save more first.

Probably you don't. The 20% down number is a myth that costs first-time buyers years.

FHA at 3.5% down on $450K is $15,750. Conventional at 5% down is $22,500. Both are real loan products that close every day in Orlando. PMI runs $200 to $300 a month until you hit 20% equity, and you get to that 20% faster than most first-time buyers expect, because home value appreciation counts toward it, not just principal paydown.

Saving toward 20% down ($90,000 on a $450K home) sounds responsible. In practice it usually means three more years of rent (around $72,000 paid to a landlord) while you scrape together another $67,500, with no guarantee the same house at the same price will be there at the end.

Here is the honest version of when waiting is the right call: your credit score is rebuildable and six months of work will move you into a meaningfully better rate tier, or your income situation is mid-change (new job, contract work that needs season-over-season verification). Those are real reasons.

Rates might drop and I need a bigger down payment usually aren't.

If something in your specific situation actually makes waiting the right call, the Buyer Strategy Session is the place to walk through it. More on that next week.

-- Axel
```
