# FTHB Quiz - Tier C - Day 35 - DTI

**Subject:** The number lenders actually care about (it isn't your score)

**Preview:** Score gets you in the door. DTI decides how much house you can buy.

**Send:** Day 35 (7 days after C4)

---

```
*|FIRST_NAME|*,

Most first-time buyers obsess over their credit score and ignore their debt-to-income ratio (DTI). Lenders do the opposite. Score gets you in the door. DTI decides the loan amount.

The fastest way to see your number is my affordability calculator, built on real Central Florida property tax and insurance instead of national averages. Enter your income and your existing monthly debts and it returns a price range plus the DTI math behind it.

[Run the affordability calculator]

Two things to know when you read the output.

First, it gives you two prices, not one. The comfortable number keeps your total debt near 36% DTI. The stretch number pushes to about 45%, which is closer to where real underwriting actually goes: most conventional loans allow back-end DTI up to 45%, FHA up to 50% in many cases. Your real ceiling usually sits between the two. The price is a range that will move; the monthly payment breakdown it shows (principal, interest, taxes, insurance, PMI) is the number to plan around.

Second, for most first-time buyers in Orlando the bottleneck is back-end DTI, not front-end. Housing payment alone usually fits inside 28%. It's housing + car payment + student loans + minimum credit card payments that pushes total debt past 45% and shrinks the loan amount.

If your back-end DTI lands above 45%, your highest-leverage move is not saving more. It's paying off the debt with the smallest balance and highest minimum. One paid-off car loan can shift back-end DTI by 5–8 points, which can be the difference between qualifying for an Orlando home in your price band and not.

-- Axel
```
