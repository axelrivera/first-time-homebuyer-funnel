Many "Ready Now" buyers in Orlando default to one of two paths:

Path 1: Buy on the Orange side (Apopka, Maitland). Apopka gives you new-construction value north of Downtown Orlando; Maitland gives you walkability and proximity to Park Avenue at Winter Park prices minus $100K. The trade-off: inventory in Maitland turns over slowly, and the commute south from Apopka stretches in peak hours.

Path 2: Push north on the Seminole side (Altamonte Springs, Longwood, Lake Mary, Winter Springs, Oviedo, Sanford). Tighter inventory in the cities at your price point and a longer Downtown Orlando commute from Sanford. The upside is consistent municipal zoning and a high share of master-planned development, which tend to produce stable price appreciation through cycles.

There's a third path most people miss: the **Seminole/Orange border zone**, where Casselberry and southern Altamonte Springs (Seminole) sit right against Maitland and northern Winter Park (Orange). Specific blocks straddle both counties' services and amenities, with meaningful price-per-square-foot differences between the two sides depending on which line you land on.

If your budget stretches into the high $500s, there's a fourth path worth a look: Lake Nona in the southeast. Newer construction, master-planned, a different mix of housing stock and amenities than anything on the north end. Worth running the math before you rule it out on geography alone.
